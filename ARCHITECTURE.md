# Architecture

How the Codex plugin records a session, and why it is built so. What a person installs and sees is the book's page, [telmoni.com/docs/api/coding-agents](https://telmoni.com/docs/api/coding-agents).

## The shape

```
Codex ──(Stop, SubagentStop, Interrupt, SessionEnd)──▶ node dist/hook.mjs
                                                          │ reads stdin, starts a detached copy, exits 0, prints nothing
                                                          ▼
                                       the copy: takes the thread's lock, reads its rollout
                                       from the saved offset, waits for the named turn's
                                       closing line while the turn goes on, cuts what it read
                                       into turns, and for each of the thread's own that has ended:
                                       RunRecord ──▶ the SDK's recordRun ──▶ POST /otlp/v1/traces
                                       then saves the offset past it
```

A turn of the agent is a run of `codex`; Codex's session id is the run's session, so a session reads in the console as a session of traces. Each response of the model is a model call, and each tool call a tool call; a subagent's turns are runs of the same session. The run's release is the Codex that wrote the rollout, its first line's `cli_version`, where that is a plain version. Codex's own OpenTelemetry export (`[otel]`) is not used: a plugin cannot set it, its logs carry the person's email and a tool's arguments, and its traces are Codex's internal span tree, with no turn's tokens, tools and outcome in one place.

## A hook that never holds the session

Codex waits for its hooks, and reads what a `Stop` hook prints as its decision: plain text is an invalid answer, on every turn, and exit 2 makes Codex carry the turn on. So the hook reads its input — the event, the session, the thread and its rollout's path, the turn and its model, nothing else — and starts a copy of itself, detached and with no stdio, handing it that input in its environment rather than its command line, which anyone on the machine can list. Then it exits 0, having printed nothing; Codex leaves a hook's detached processes running once it has returned, so a slow network or a Telmoni that does not answer never slows a turn. Codex closes a hook's stdin once it has written its input; one left open is given up on after two seconds, so the hook still returns well inside the three seconds Codex gives its shortest. Whatever goes wrong after is a line in `hook.log`, never an error the session shows.

Each hook is given ten seconds at `Stop` and `SubagentStop`, and three, the most Codex allows, at `Interrupt` and `SessionEnd`; it needs a fraction of one. Its command, `node "${PLUGIN_ROOT}/dist/hook.mjs"`, names no version and no shell's variable — Codex puts the plugin's folder in itself — since Codex asks the person to trust a hook again whenever its command changes.

## Reading a rollout

A rollout is JSON Lines that Codex appends as a thread goes (`~/.codex/sessions/…/rollout-…-<thread>.jsonl`), in one of two dialects: legacy, or paginated (`session_meta.history_mode`), which numbers its records and writes a completion for every item. The copy reads whole lines forward from the thread's saved offset, four megabytes at a time, so a rollout grown to gigabytes is never read from its start twice; a last line without its newline is still being written, and waits for the next read. Each line is parsed, and what the plugin keeps of it is picked out by field there and then: its kind and time, a turn's id and outcome, a model's name, Codex's token counts, a tool call's id and name, and its completion's status, exit code and times. A line past 32 megabytes, only ever a tool's output or a compaction's summary, is passed over unparsed.

**Turns.** A turn opens at `task_started` and closes at its `task_complete` or `turn_aborted`; one Codex left open closes at the next turn's opening. Nothing outside a turn opens one, so the closing line of a turn already sent, or a fork's boundary for a turn whose opening is in another rollout, is passed over.

**When a turn has ended.** A turn has ended at its closing line, and at `Stop` and `SubagentStop` the turn the hook names has ended too: Codex runs those hooks before it writes the turn's `task_complete`, which follows once every `Stop` hook has returned — another plugin's, which Codex gives ten minutes unless told otherwise — and any compaction Codex runs at a turn's end is done; a `Stop` hook that blocks has Codex carry the turn on, its lines still coming. So the copy waits for that line, for the turn's outcome and its last calls, while the turn's lines come and for ten minutes past the last, and sends the turn as it stands if the line does not come; another turn's lines are no reason to wait. A turn a `Stop` hook named is done even where Codex, dying, never closed it and the next turn's opening did. `Interrupt` names a turn the person cut short, before Codex writes its `turn_aborted`; the copy waits for that line too, and sends the turn cancelled either way. A turn an error ended, a command run with `!` and a `/compact` get no hook of their own: the next hook sends them, or `SessionEnd`, which also ends whatever was left open as cancelled. Codex names a turn's error on its closing line from 0.145 on; before it, a turn an error ended closes as one that completed, and is recorded so.

**What is a run.** A turn holding the model's work is a run. A command the person ran with `!` holds none, and is passed over; a compaction holds the call that summarized the context, and is a run of that one call.

**Responses and tokens.** The model's output — its reasoning, its replies, its calls — is one response until something goes back to the model, a tool's answer or a prompt, after which its next output is the next response. A response's tokens are Codex's record of it, `token_usage_record`, written as the response completed (Codex 0.153 on); where Codex wrote none, its share of the running total, `token_count`, which Codex writes once the response's tools have run. A share counts only where the total is new, since Codex writes the total again whenever its rate limits refresh, and only where it reads true, since after a compaction it is an estimate, zero but for its total; a response is never given both. OpenAI counts input with its cached tokens inside it and output with its reasoning inside it, as the SDK takes them: a call's input is `input_tokens`, its cache's reads `cached_input_tokens` and writes `cache_write_input_tokens`, and its output `output_tokens`.

**Tool calls.** A function call, a custom tool's call (`apply_patch`), a local shell's, a tool search, a web search and an image generation are each a tool call by name, an MCP tool's as `mcp__<server>__<tool>` from Codex's own record of the call. Paginated history records each tool's completion: a command's status and exit code, an MCP tool's, a patch's or a dynamic tool's status. A call failed there is failed (`ToolError`), and one the person declined, or a subagent interrupted, cancelled. Legacy history records an MCP tool's outcome and a patch's, but a command's exit code only inside its output's text, which is never read: such a command is recorded as run. A call never answered, its turn interrupted or ended first, is cancelled.

**Times.** A line's time is when Codex wrote it. A response starts as the line before its first was written, the request going out then, and ends as its record was written, or with its last output. A tool call runs from when Codex started it to when it finished where Codex records those, and otherwise from the model's call to its answer. A turn runs from its opening to its closing line, or to its last line where it has none; and, where a clock set back mid-turn wrote a call before the opening or after the close, from its first call's start to its last call's end. No span ends before it starts.

**What ingest takes.** Ingest refuses a span whole for a name past its cap — a tool's past 145 characters, a model's past 128 — and an export whole for text it cannot decode, a count no integer, or a time outside the nanoseconds OTLP counts in 64 bits; a refusal is the same on every hook. So a lone surrogate never leaves the reader in a name, a count past what a number holds exactly reads as none, a time outside the epoch and 2200 as no time, and a name past its cap is cut to it, never inside a character, after its spaces are trimmed. A tool's name is written by the model, so one that is not a plain name (letters, digits and `_ . : @ + -`), as a local model may write text or a path in its place, is sent as `unknown`. A local model's name, set in a person's configuration, may be a file's path, which never leaves: a path to a model's weights (`.gguf`, `.ggml`, `.bin`, `.safetensors`, `.llamafile`) is sent as the file's name, which is the model's, and any other path — from the root, the home folder, a drive, `.` or `..`, or a `file:` URL — as `unknown`. The suites hold every span they send to ingest's rules.

## Forks and subagents

A subagent Codex spawns works in a thread of its own, its rollout its own (`agent_transcript_path` at `SubagentStop`), and its turns are runs of the session that spawned it. A thread forked or spawned from another may begin with the other's history: a legacy fork copies its source's turns, the source's turn still running closed by a boundary the fork writes, and a subagent's rollout begins with its parent's history, which a paginated rollout numbers below `subagent_history_start_ordinal`. Those turns are the source's, sent by the source's own hooks, and never again: nothing below that ordinal is read, and in a thread forked or spawned from another, a turn whose id — a version 7 UUID, minted as the turn began — is older than the thread itself is passed over. A paginated fork leaves its source's history in the source (`history_base`) and holds only its own.

## Ids, and sending each turn once

A run's id is a version 5 UUID of the session's id and the turn's, under the plugin's own namespace, so a turn read twice is the same run, before an update and after it. The SDK derives every span's id from the run's id and its place in the record, and queues a record in one synchronous pass, so the same turn sent again is the same bytes; ingest's claim on a body answers a second copy as it answered the first, for an hour. Past that hour, or as other bytes, a copy is stored again — ingest keeps spans as they come — which is why the state is saved as soon after each turn as it is.

Each turn goes in an export of its own, flushed before the next. The SDK queues a record whole, so a run holds at most 10,000 calls, its earliest, the rest left out and logged, and the SDK's queue is sized to hold one, past its own 2,048 spans. The SDK sends a record in batches of 512 spans, one at a time, and a batch can take 150 seconds — three attempts of ten seconds, and a wait of a minute at most between each two — so the flush is given 150 seconds for each batch and 30 more: it ends on the platform's answer, and a turn the platform took late is never counted as not sent and stored again past the hour. A turn is done with once the SDK reports no warning that it could not deliver the spans: the platform took it, whole or less a span refused or an attribute dropped, or refused it for good — for what it holds (`400`, `413`), for a fault of its own (a `500`, but for its database's, `/errors/internal/database`, which a later hook may find mended), for an organization paused (`402`) — which the same bytes would get on every hook: logged, it holds up no turn after it, nor does a record the SDK itself refuses. The SDK's warnings name statuses, problem types, span ids and attribute keys, never anything of the session, so they are logged as they are; the SDK warns of each kind once a client, so each turn goes through a client of its own, and a turn refused as the one before it is never read as taken. The state is saved past a turn taken, at most once a second while a long rollout is read, and always at the end. A turn the platform did not take — Telmoni out of reach, busy or too slow, its database failing it, its key or endpoint refused — is read again from its opening by the next hook. A session's end is its last hook, so where it met such a turn it leaves `<session>.left` beside the state, and any later hook that reached the platform with its own turns then ends those sessions again, the longest waiting first, its own thread and its subagents', each lock taken without waiting, stopping at the first turn still not taken. The turns sent are kept with the offset, so a rollout read again from its start sends none of them twice.

A lock per thread (`<session>.lock`, `<session>.<thread>.lock`), freshened every ten seconds by the hook holding it and taken as left by a hook that died once a minute stale, keeps a `Stop`, an `Interrupt` and a `SessionEnd` from sending the same turns at once; a `Stop`, `SubagentStop` or `Interrupt` that waits twelve minutes without it, past a quiet wait for a closing line, gives up, and the next hook sends; a `SessionEnd`, after which no hook comes, waits as long as a live hook holds it, since a hook sends every ended turn and no fixed wait is sure to outlast one. A hook writes a token of its own into the lock and lets go of it only while it holds that token, so one left stale by a sleep of the machine never frees the lock another hook took from it.

## Where things are kept

Beside the folder Codex runs with (`CODEX_HOME`, `~/.codex` by default), in `telmoni/`: each thread's state — `<session>.json` for the session's own thread, `<session>.<thread>.json` for a subagent's, so the session's end finds its subagents' — holding the offset, the rollout it is into and that file's identity, the turns sent, the running total, the model the rollout last named and the turn that last opened before the offset, and where, and whether a missing key, a key file passed over or a refused endpoint was logged; the locks; and `hook.log`, moved to `hook.log.1` past a megabyte. All of it is created mode `600`, in a folder created mode `700`; a folder that is there already keeps its own. At the session's end a subagent's state that cannot be read is logged and passed over, holding up none of the others'. A state is written aside, synced and renamed over the last, so none is left torn; one that cannot be read, where it is not simply missing, fails the hook, logged under its thread and left alone, since read as new it would send the thread again from the top, and past ingest's hour store its turns twice. A thread moved to a new rollout, as a revert moves one, a rollout replaced or shrunk, and one rewritten where it lies — no line ending at the offset, or that turn no longer opening where it did — is read again from its start. Codex's `PLUGIN_DATA` would keep the state as well — it too outlives an update — but `~/.codex/telmoni/` is where a person looks, as `~/.claude/telmoni/` is for the Claude Code plugin.

The key and the endpoint are `TELMONI_API_KEY` and `TELMONI_ENDPOINT` in the environment Codex hands its hooks — Codex's own, the `.env` in its folder (`~/.codex/.env`) loaded into it — and, where those are unset, the plugin's own file, `telmoni.json` in the folder Codex runs with (`{ "apiKey", "endpoint" }`, mode `600`): Codex keeps no settings and no secrets for a plugin's hooks. The file is read only where it is the person's own and shut to everyone else, checked on the descriptor it is read from, as `ssh` reads a key, since one others could write could name an endpoint of theirs; past one not used — a field in it empty or no string among them — nothing is sent, the log saying why once, since the endpoint it may have named would otherwise give way to the default. The log, which holds no path, names it `~/.codex/telmoni.json`, or `$CODEX_HOME/telmoni.json` where that is set. The no-key, key-file and refused-endpoint lines are written under the thread's lock, so no hook's save is lost to them, with the rollout's path, so the session's end finds a subagent that stopped before there was a key. Without a key nothing is sent, and the log says so once per thread for each reason; so too for an endpoint the SDK refuses — no `http` or `https` URL, or plain HTTP to another machine — which the log names in the SDK's own words, which name at most its host, the thread's turns waiting until it changes. The provider is the rollout's `model_provider` (`openai`), Codex's own name for it, but where the GenAI conventions and the price table name that service otherwise: Codex's `azure` goes as `azure.ai.openai`.

## What ships

The npm package `@telmoni/codex-observability-plugin` holds `.codex-plugin/plugin.json`, `hooks/hooks.json` and `dist/hook.mjs` — tsup's one bundle of the plugin, the SDK and OpenTelemetry — beside `package.json` and `LICENSE`, which npm always adds, and nothing else: npm would add a README beside `package.json` too, whatever `files` says, so the README is `.github/README.md`, which GitHub shows on the repository's page. Codex fetches it with the person's own npm (`npm pack --ignore-scripts`), installs no dependencies and runs none of its scripts; the hook needs Node 22.23.4, 24.22.1, 26.12.1 or later on `PATH`, October 2026's security releases, as `engines` says, though the built hook has run end to end on 22.0.0; and nothing installed. It has run on Linux alone so far: on Windows, the detached copy, the paths and `node` on `PATH` are untried. The marketplace is this repository's `.agents/plugins/marketplace.json`: it names the package, so Codex clones this repository for the catalog and fetches the plugin as built from npm, never a build of the clone; plugins with hooks are kept out of OpenAI's own directory, so the repository is its own marketplace. Codex 0.143 is the first to fetch a plugin from npm; `SessionEnd` came with 0.145 and `Interrupt` with 0.150, and an older Codex passes over a hook for an event it does not have.

**A release** is the version bumped in one commit on `main` — `package.json`, both of `package-lock.json`'s and `.codex-plugin/plugin.json`'s — and nothing else by hand. Its push starts `.github/workflows/release.yml`, which waits for CI to pass on that commit, verifies and packs the package as CI does, and waits for the owner's approval in its `release` environment: one job tags the commit `v*`, and another stages the package on npm by trusted publishing, with provenance, so no token is stored anywhere. The GitHub release follows, and the version goes live once the owner approves it in the package's *Staged Packages* tab on npmjs.com. `0.1.0` and `0.1.1` went up by hand, untagged; `0.1.2` and `0.1.3` came from CI on 23 October 2026, tagged `v0.1.2` and `v0.1.3`.

The SDK is a development dependency, the registry's `telmoni`, bundled at build.

## Testing

`tests/` runs the reader, the turns and the record over synthetic rollouts (`tests/fixtures/`, written from the record shapes in Codex's source, every text in them fake): both dialects, two forks and a subagent, records and fields Codex does not write yet, clocks set back, names, counts and times past what ingest takes, and a local model named by its file's path; the session's hook against a fake sender, over rollouts written as Codex writes them, a closing line arriving while the hook waits and a turn carried on past it, a turn the platform did not take, two hooks of a thread at once, rollouts replaced, shrunk, rewritten in place and moved, and one of many megabytes; the lock, taken from a hook that died, never from one at work, never freed by a hook it was taken from, given up at a stop and outwaited at the session's end; a state that cannot be read, never taken for a new one, and a subagent's passed over at the session's end; the key file read only where it is shut to all but its owner, and passed over for a field empty or no string; the hook's input as Codex hands it, a subagent's among it, and what it refuses; the sender through the real SDK to a fake ingest, a turn sent again after a crash, a refusal and an outage the same bytes, a turn refused for good passed over and one refused for its key or its database's fault kept, each turn's refusal heard through a client of its own, a flush's bound, an endpoint the SDK refuses named once and its turns sent once it takes one, ingest's answers of what it kept, and a turn past the SDK's own queue; the manifests and the package as Codex reads them, since Codex ships no validator; and the built hook as Codex runs it, a process handed its input on stdin that must print nothing and exit at once while its copy sends, its command line holding neither key nor input, an endpoint the SDK refuses named in its log once, a state it cannot read logged under its thread and left as it is, and its every span within ingest's rules.
